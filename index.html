Biochemistry you can actually see.

Interactive metabolic maps and plain-language clinical write-ups, built from NYCPM lecture material. Pan, zoom, and switch pathways on and off until the picture makes sense.

Biochemistry cycles

Gluconeogenesis, glycolysis, PDH, the Krebs cycle, lactate and ethanol fermentation, the 2,3-BPG shunt and the malate–aspartate shuttle on one map — with every enzyme, cofactor, activator and inhibitor labelled.

Clinical explanations

Short write-ups connecting the pathways to disease: enzyme deficiencies, poisonings, lab findings and the questions that show up on exams.

One consistent visual language

A boxed × marks an irreversible step, a star marks a rate-limiting step, and every new pathway added to the map follows the same colour and symbol rules.

This is an unofficial student project. It is not produced, reviewed or endorsed by the New York College of Podiatric Medicine. Always check your own lecture material before an exam.

Carbohydrate metabolism map

Gluconeogenesis runs up the left, glycolysis runs down the right, and they meet at glucose and pyruvate. Pyruvate carboxylase is drawn once: a single oxaloacetate branch point that feeds both the Krebs cycle and gluconeogenesis. Quantities are per glucose (×2 below the aldolase split).
